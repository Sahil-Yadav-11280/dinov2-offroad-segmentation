OFF-ROAD SEMANTIC SEGMENTATION USING TRANSFORMER-BASED FEATURE REPRESENTATIONS

1.  Abstract
This work presents a semantic segmentation framework for off-road terrain understanding using a pretrained DINOv2 Vision Transformer backbone combined with a lightweight convolutional segmentation head. The model performs dense pixel-wise classification across 10 terrain classes and demonstrates stable convergence under limited training time.

2.  Introduction
Accurate semantic segmentation of off-road environments is critical for autonomous navigation systems. Terrain variability, vegetation density, and object diversity make pixel-level classification challenging.

To address this, we leverage pretrained transformer-based visual representations (DINOv2) to extract robust high-level image features and train a dedicated segmentation head for terrain classification.

3.  Methodology
   Backbone:
    DINOv2 (ViT-S/14 pretrained)
    Patch-based feature extraction
    Frozen during training

   Segmentation Head:
    ConvNeXt-style convolutional decoder
    Maps transformer embeddings to spatial predictions
    Produces 10-class dense output

   Loss Function:
    Cross-Entropy Loss

   Optimizer:
    SGD

   Evaluation Metrics:
    Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU)
    Dice Coefficient
    Pixel Accuracy

4.  Dataset and Classes
The model predicts the following terrain categories:

	1.  Background
	2.  Trees
	3.  Lush Bushes
	4.  Dry Grass
	5.  Dry Bushes
	6.  Ground Clutter
	7.  Logs
	8.  Rocks
	9.  Landscape
	10.  Sky

5.  Experimental Setup
Input Resolution: 480 x 960 (aligned with patch size multiple of 14) Batch Size: 2 Epochs: 10 (with additional fine-	tuning) Training Strategy: Frozen backbone with supervised segmentation head training

6.  Results
	Final Validation Metrics:
	Mean IoU: 0.2918
	Dice Score: 0.4349
	Pixel Accuracy: 0.7019
	
	Training exhibited consistent improvement across epochs with no observable overfitting.

	Qualitative results show accurate segmentation of dominant terrain classes such as background and sky, with moderate 	performance on smaller objects such as logs and rocks.

7.  Discussion
The transformer backbone provides strong global contextual representations, enabling stable learning despite limited training time. Performance is influenced by class imbalance, particularly affecting minority terrain categories.

Future improvements may include:

   Class-weighted loss functions
   Hybrid Dice + Cross-Entropy loss
   Extended training schedule
   Data augmentation strategies
   Backbone fine-tuning

8.  Conclusion
This study demonstrates the effectiveness of leveraging pretrained transformer-based representations for off-road semantic segmentation. The model achieves stable convergence and reasonable performance under constrained computational resources, highlighting the practicality of transfer learning in terrain understanding applications.
